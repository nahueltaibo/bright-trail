## Kinds

- **math**: wrong or doubtful math, or a rule that teaches a misconception.
- **grade**: outside the grade or the skill.
- **ambiguous**: wording a child could read two ways.
- **tone**: too easy, too hard, or a difficulty label.
- **narration**: hard to follow by ear, or the picture and the words disagree.
- **other**: weak distractors, giveaways, cosmetic wording.

## What Was Left, and Why

- **TTS worries.** 20 notes say the voice reads "1/3" as "one slash three". It doesn't: `toSpeech` turns fractions, mixed numbers, and percents into words before speaking.
- **One picture per card or item.** Comparisons like "Pip has 7, Mia has 5" show one group. Both amounts are spoken, so they play by ear.
- **Choice order.** The app shuffles choices, so narration can't follow screen order, and position never gives the answer away.
- **Percent in Grade 5.** Ontario Grade 5 B1.7 introduces whole-number percents, so `g5-fraction-decimal-percent` stays.
- **Cosmetic.** Weak distractors, difficulty labels, scaffolds, and grammar slips that don't change what a child learns.

## For the Owner

- Number-line items depend on seeing the line (notes 127 and new 21). Speaking the frog's position as it moves would fix all of them in the app.
- New note 30: `g3-fraction-repeated-addition-keypad-5` counts 6 fourths over two wholes. Check that improper fractions fit Grade 3.
- The fraction prompt tells the generator to name a picture choice by its fraction, such as "1/3 of the circle". That gave away "which picture shows 1/3?" in 4 items, now fixed by hand. Rewording the prompt to "1 of 3 parts shaded" would regenerate all 14 fraction skills, so it waits for the next content pass.
