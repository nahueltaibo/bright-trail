### OE15

As children progress through the Kindergarten program, they:

**OE15.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers, using concrete materials to explore and investigate counting, quantity, and number relationships

#### Conceptual understandings

- Numbers represent a common organizational structure that we use in our lives and in our world to communicate/represent value.
- Numbers can be taken apart (decomposed) and put together (recomposed).
- The ability to decompose and recompose is a useful strategy in all aspects of mathematical thinking.
- We can use objects, pictures, symbols, and/or words to represent number and quantity.
- There are many ways to count. Each way to count has a proper sequence.
- Quantity can be represented in many ways.
- The same quantity can look different (concept of abstraction).
- We are learning that as we move up or down the counting sequence, the quantity increases or decreases by the number we are counting by (concept of magnitude).

#### Specific expectations

As children progress through the Kindergarten program, they:

**15.1** investigate (e.g., using a number line, a hundreds carpet, a board game with numbered squares) the idea that a number’s position in the counting sequence determines its magnitude (e.g., the quantity is greater when counting forward and less when counting backward)

**15.2** investigate some concepts of quantity and equality through identifying and comparing sets with more, fewer, or the same number of objects (e.g., find out which of two cups contains more or fewer beans [i.e., the concept of one-to-one correspondence]; investigate the ideas of more, less, or the same, using concrete materials such as counters or five and ten frames; recognize that the last number counted represents the number of objects in the set [i.e., the concept of cardinality])

**15.3** make use of one-to-one correspondence in counting objects and matching groups of objects

**15.4** demonstrate an understanding of the counting concepts of stable order (i.e., the concept that the counting sequence is always the same – 1 is followed by 2, 2 by 3, and so on) and of order irrelevance (i.e., the concept that the number of objects in a set will be the same regardless of which object is used to begin the counting)

**15.5** subitize quantities to 5 without having to count, using a variety of materials (e.g., dominoes, dot plates, dice, number of fingers) and strategies (e.g., composing or decomposing numbers)

**15.6** use information to estimate the number in a small set (e.g., apply knowledge of quantity; use a common reference such as a five frame; subitize)

**15.7** explore and communicate the function/purpose of numbers in a variety of contexts (e.g., use magnetic and sandpaper numerals to represent the number of objects in a set [to indicate quantity]; line up toys and manipulatives, and identify the first, second, and so on [to indicate ordinality]; use footsteps to discover the distance between the door and the sink [to measure]; identify a favourite sports player: “My favourite player is number twenty-four” [to label or name])

**15.8** explore different Canadian coins, using coin manipulatives (e.g., role-play the purchasing of items at the store in the dramatic play area; determine which coin will purchase more – a loonie or a quarter)

**15.9** compose and decompose quantities to 10 (e.g., make multiple representations of numbers using two or more colours of linking cubes, blocks, dot strips, and other manipulatives; play “shake and spill” games)

**15.10** investigate addition and subtraction in everyday experiences and routines through the use of modelling strategies and manipulatives (e.g., join two sets of objects, one containing a greater number than the other, and count all the objects; separate out the smaller number of objects and determine how many remain) and counting strategies (e.g., use a counting sequence to determine how many objects there are altogether; count backward from the largest number to determine how many objects remain)

See also OE20: SE20.1 and SE20.2

### OE20

As children progress through the Kindergarten program, they:

**OE20.** apply the mathematical processes to support the development of mathematical thinking, to demonstrate understanding, and to communicate thinking and learning in mathematics, while engaged in play-based learning and in other contexts

Official note: "The specific expectations in the following chart are used as examples to illustrate that the mathematical processes are relevant to and embedded in all expectations that relate to demonstrating mathematics behaviours, regardless of their particular focus (e.g., on number sense and numeration or measurement or geometry and spatial sense)."

#### Specific expectations (number only)

As children progress through the Kindergarten program, they:

**20.1** demonstrate an understanding of number relationships for numbers from 0 to 10, through investigation (e.g., show small quantities using fingers or manipulatives)

**20.2** use, read, and represent whole numbers to 10 in a variety of meaningful contexts (e.g., use a hundreds chart to read whole numbers; use magnetic and sandpaper numerals to represent the number of objects in a set; put the house number on a house built in the blocks area; find and recognize numbers in the environment; write numerals on imaginary bills at the restaurant in the dramatic play area)

### A6. Number Sense and Operations

As students progress through Kindergarten, they:

**A6.** use numbers and operations

#### Specific expectations

##### A6.1

**A6.1** explore what numbers are used for

**Examples**

**Different uses of numbers:**

- **to indicate quantity or cardinality:** a student uses magnetic and sandpaper numerals to represent the number of objects in a set
- **to indicate ordinality:** a student lines up toys and manipulatives, and identifies the first, second, and so on
- **to label or name:** “My favourite player is number 24.”

##### A6.2

**A6.2** understand when counting forward, the quantity gets bigger and when counting backward, the quantity gets smaller

**Examples**

**Tools to support an understanding of the relationship between quantity and a number’s placement in a counting sequence:**

- walkable number paths
- concrete vertical number paths built with stacking blocks or connecting cubes
- number path games and board games with numbered squares

##### A6.3

**A6.3** identify and write numbers to 20

**Examples**

**Identifying numbers:**

- recognizing numbers represented with numerals and concrete representations, including those using fingers or manipulatives, five and ten frames, rekenreks, and bead strings

**Numbers:**

- identifying and recording numbers found throughout the indoor and outdoor environments

##### A6.4

**A6.4** subitize quantities to 5

**Examples**

**Materials and strategies to develop subitizing skills:**

- dominoes, single-colour dot cards/plates, two-colour dot cards/plates, dice, five and ten frames, playing cards, and fingers
- games with dice, playing cards, or dot cards

##### A6.5

**A6.5** compose and decompose quantities to 10

**Examples**

**Materials and strategies to develop composing and decomposing skills:**

- make multiple representations of numbers using two colours of connecting cubes, blocks, counters in five and ten frames, rekenreks, playing cards, and other manipulatives
- do activities that involve part-whole and missing-part relationships, including Shake and Spill with two-colour counters and Race to 10 using a ten frame, counters, and a spinner

##### A6.6

**A6.6** understand the concept of more than, the same (equal), or less than

**Examples**

**Materials and strategies for investigating relationships between quantities:**

- use concrete materials and tools, including rekenreks and counters with five and ten frames
- use matching and counting strategies to find out which of two cups contains more buttons

##### A6.7

**A6.7** estimate the number of objects in small collections and check by counting

**Examples**

**Estimation strategies:**

- use a common referent, such as a five or ten frame, to make comparisons, such as “Is the quantity closer to 5 or 10?”
- estimate the quantity of an unknown collection by comparing it to a benchmark (known) collection
- use conceptual subitizing strategies for small quantities

##### A6.8

**A6.8** count to 20 by 1s, starting from any number

**Examples**

- use songs, rhymes, chants, and/or speaking to explore number and counting sequences

##### A6.9

**A6.9** solve equal-share problems using concrete materials

**Examples**

**Equal-share problem:**

- There are 4 stickers and 2 friends. How many stickers will each friend get if they share the stickers equally?

##### A6.10

**A6.10** describe relationships between any two numbers from 0 to 20

**Examples**

**Number relationships:**

- greater than or less than, one and two more/less

**Tools for identifying number relationships:**

- five and ten frames, rekenreks, part-whole models such as two-colour dot cards

**Strategies for identifying number relationships:**

- use tally marks
- count on fingers
- use the benchmarks 5 and 10
- make a 5 and a 10 using concrete materials

##### A6.11

**A6.11** recall addition facts to 5 and related subtraction facts

**Examples**

**Developing addition facts:**

- play number memory games using dot cards/plates that go up to 5 and have dots in two colours
- introduce strategies of “one or two more”, counting on, and “doubles”

**Developing subtraction facts:**

- play games with a given whole and a missing part
- introduce strategies of “one or two less” and counting back

##### A6.12

**A6.12** solve situations involving addition and subtraction to 10 using concrete materials

**Examples**

**Situations:**

- **join problems:** A friend has 2 apples. You give her 1 more. How many apples does she have?
- **separating problems:** You have 5 apples. You give 1 to a friend. How many apples do you have now?
- **part-whole problems:** You have 7 apples. Five of them are red and the rest are green. How many green apples do you have?
- **compare problems:** You have 8 apples. Your friend has 4 apples. Who has more? By how much?

**Tools and strategies:**

- use five and ten frames, connecting cubes, rekenreks, number paths, or fingers to represent the quantities in the situation
- act out the situation using strategies such as counting all, counting on, counting back, or finding the difference (for subtraction)
- use one and two more/less
- use the benchmarks 5 and 10

##### A6.13

**A6.13** identify and explore the value of Canadian coins to 25¢ and coins and bills to $10

**Examples**

- use sorting trays or hoops for organizing coin manipulatives and play money
