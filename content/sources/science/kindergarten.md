## Strand B: Problem Solving and Innovating

### Why is this learning important?

Learning to think creatively and critically is important in all aspects of life. Your child learns about the world around them and thinks in new ways to solve problems.
Your child will learn to:

- explore the world through natural curiosity, in ways that engage their mind, senses and body
- ask questions
- try things out
- design or make something new or different

### B11. Coding Skills

**B11.** use foundational coding concepts and skills

#### B11.1

**B11.1** use directional and positional language to create and follow instructions involving movement

**Examples**

- directional language: “Move forward three steps, then turn left and move forward one step.”
- positional language: “Move to the closest red square, then move to the blue square beside the red square.”

#### B11.2

**B11.2** test and refine instructions

**Examples**

- when students review a picture story illustrating the sequence of steps for preparing to go outside for recess, they notice that one step is out of order and work together to correct it
- when students program a robot to move across the room to the door, the robot hits the wall instead, so they work together to revise the instructions to ensure the robot reaches the door

#### B11.3

**B11.3** communicate and record step-by-step instructions using symbols, words, or pictures

**Examples**

- use arrows to give a sequential set of instructions on how to move across a grid
- use clear and precise language to outline the sequence of steps needed to build a structure using blocks
- use picture cards to sequence a story

### B12. Scientific Investigation and Engineering Design

**B12.** use an inquiry process to engage in scientific investigations and to design, build, and test models and structures

#### B12.1

**B12.1** state problems and pose questions

**Examples**

- state the following problem when building a block tower: “Our tower keeps falling over. What can we do to make it stand up better?”
- notice that some objects don’t stick to a magnet and ask why certain materials are attracted to magnets while others are not

#### B12.2

**B12.2** make predictions and observations while exploring, investigating, and designing

**Examples**

- predict that a heavier object will sink faster than a lighter one, and then observe how different materials behave in water during a sink-or-float investigation
- predict that a bumpy surface will slow down a toy car more than a smooth one, and then observe how the car moves across sandpaper, carpet, and tile

#### B12.3

**B12.3** select materials and tools to carry out investigations and to construct models and structures

**Examples**

- use different fabrics and droppers to test which materials absorb water best
- select sticks, leaves, and string to build a model of a bird’s nest after observing one outdoors
- use magnets, paper clips, and cardboard to design a structure for a simple game that uses magnetic force
- gather small containers, funnels, and sand to explore how different shapes affect pouring and flow

#### B12.4

**B12.4** draw conclusions and communicate results

**Examples**

- notice patterns and draw conclusions from a plant growth investigation
- show or describe the process of building their structure

#### B12.5

**B12.5** design, build, and test models and structures

**Examples**

- design and build a shelter for a small toy animal, and then test whether it will stay dry when water is gently poured over it
- construct towers using paper of a variety of types, and then test the towers’ stability by blowing on them
- create a model of a spiderweb using yarn and sticks, and then test its strength by placing small objects on it

#### B12.6

**B12.6** follow safety rules to help prevent injuries, including concussion, and demonstrate an understanding of the importance of these practices

**Examples**

- remind a peer to take turns using the climbing structure to prevent bumping into each other while testing their parachute models
- notice a patch of ice on the playground and inform an educator to help keep others safe while building snow forts

### B13. Exploring Environments

**B13.** investigate natural and built environments

#### B13.1

**B13.1** ask questions about and describe some natural occurrences

**Examples**

**Student questions:**

- “Why does the Moon change shape?”
- “Where does the wind come from?”

**Student descriptions:**

- “The clouds are moving fast because it’s windy today.”
- “The snow melted because the Sun came out and it’s warmer.”

#### B13.2

**B13.2** sort and classify groups of living and non-living things

**Examples**

**Sorting categories:**

- things that grow and things that do not grow
- things that are liquid and things that are solid

**Sorting tools:**

- hula hoops, sorting circles, paper plates, T-charts, Venn diagrams

#### B13.3

**B13.3** compare patterns in natural and built environments

**Examples**

**Compare:**

- the spiral of a snail shell with a spiral staircase
- the symmetry of butterfly wings with the symmetry of a stained-glass window design
- stripes on animals with painted lines on the street
