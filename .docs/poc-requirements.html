1. What we want to learn

One question: does assess → teach → check help a real kid learn, and will they come back to it? Everything not needed to answer that is out.

HypothesisHow we measure itPass bar
Kids want to use itSessions per week, and whether they ask for itEach kid does 3+ sessions a week for 3 weeks, with no more than one reminder per session
Placement finds the right levelA parent checks 10 placed skills per kid by handThe parent agrees on 8 of 10
The loop teachesSkills moving from gap to mastered, then passing review5+ skills mastered per kid. 70% of them still pass the 1-week review.
Walk-back finds hidden gapsAssumed skills that fail a probeThe app finds and fixes at least one real earlier gap. A parent confirms it was real.
Audio-first works for a non-readerParent watches the 6-year-old's first 5 sessions3 of 5 sessions finished without reading help
AI content is good enough without educatorsItems flagged as wrong or confusingNo more than 1 factual error per 50 items

3. Scope

In

  • One strand. Ontario Mathematics 2020, Strand B (Number), Kindergarten to Grade 6. Roughly 80 skills.
  • Skill graph with assumed mastery and walk-back, as in the requirements. It's the core idea, so it stays.
  • Placement assessment, adaptive, per kid.
  • One nugget format: a narrated story of 5–8 cards.
  • Three item types: tap the answer (with pictures), number keypad, drag on a number line.
  • Mastery check and spaced review at 2 days and 1 week.
  • Audio-first, with a switch to reading mode.
  • Parent screen behind a PIN.

Out

  • Other strands and subjects, French, and interest themes.
  • Accounts, a backend, consent flow, and compliance work. All data stays on your devices.
  • AI calls at runtime. All content is generated and validated before testing.
  • Video, mini-game engines beyond the three item types, and AI images.
  • BKT, IRT, and Elo. A simple pass rule is enough for two kids.
  • App store publishing.

4. How a session works

A session is 10 minutes at most, once a day per kid. The first one or two sessions are placement.

  S --> C{Check
4 of 5 right?}
  C -- yes --> OK[Mastered
review in 2 days]
  C -- no, first time --> S
  C -- no, second time --> W[Walk back

  W --> N
  OK --> E[Stars on the trail map]
  • Pass rule: 4 of 5 items right in a check. A review is 3 items and needs all 3 right.
  • Walk-back: two failed checks mark the skill a gap and probe its prerequisites. An assumed prerequisite that fails becomes the next skill to teach.
  • Placement: about 15 minutes, split into two sessions if the kid tires. No scores shown.

5. Content, built ahead of time

A script on your computer builds the whole content bundle once. The app ships with it, so it needs no network and no API key.

the graph]
  C --> D[Skill graph JSON]
  D --> E[LLM writes per skill:
1 story nugget, 15 items]
  E --> F[Validate
schema, math solver,
reading level, second model]
  F -- fail --> E
  F -- pass --> G[Content bundle JSON]
  G --> H[Bundled into the app]
  • 15 items per skill: enough for placement probes, two checks, and two reviews without repeats.
  • Models: Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes content. Claude Haiku 5.5 validates.
  • Math answers are checked by code, not by a model.
  • You skim the graph before testing. It's about 80 skills and takes an evening. That's not educator review, just a sanity check.
  • Fixing content: flagged items get fixed in the bundle and the app rebuilt.

6. App requirements

Kid side

  • Pick a profile by avatar. No typing, no reading needed.
  • Narration reads every card, question, and answer choice. Tapping a speaker button replays it.
  • Reading mode switch in the kid's settings turns narration off by default.
  • Big tap targets, one question per screen, instant feedback with a short "why" on a miss.
  • A flag button on every card and item: "This looks wrong".
  • Trail map with stars as the only reward. No streaks, no timers.

Parent side

  • PIN-gated parent screen.
  • Add a child: first name, birthdate, avatar. The app shows the derived grade, and the parent can change it.
  • Skill tree per child, colored by state: assumed, gap, learning, mastered, review due.
  • Placement review: list of placed skills so a parent can spot-check 10.
  • Flagged content list.
  • Export all attempts as a JSON file through the share sheet, for analysis after the test.
  • Reset a child's progress.

Data logged per attempt

Child, skill, item, answer, correct or not, time to answer, replays of narration, session ID, timestamp. Enough to check every pass bar in section 1.

1-week reviews, export logs]

  • Dry run: both parents play as each kid first, deliberately missing answers to trigger walk-back.
  • Week 1: sit with each kid. Note where the 6-year-old needs reading help and where either kid gets bored.
  • Weeks 2–3: no prompting beyond one reminder. Keep a short daily note of what each kid said about it.
  • Week 4: let reviews come due, then export logs and score the table in section 1.

9. Risks

  • The 10-year-old finds it babyish. Story style should vary by grade. Watch the first sessions closely.
  • Device voice is too robotic for the 6-year-old. Fallback: pre-record audio with a cloud TTS during the content build.
  • The kids are your own. They may play to please you, and you know their level already. Treat engagement results as a ceiling, not a forecast.
  • Strand B is mostly arithmetic. Results may not carry over to Language or Science.
  • Placement fatigue for the 6-year-old. Split placement into two sessions if needed.
