BrightTrail — Subjects

What Ontario teaches from Kindergarten to Grade 6, where each program lives, and which minigames fit each subject · Draft v0.1 · 2026-11-12

Today BrightTrail covers one strand of one subject: Math, Strand B (Number). The rest of Math and four more subjects fit the app well. Two subjects fit only in part, because most of their learning is done with the body or out loud. Six new minigames would cover most of what's left, since each one serves several subjects.

1. Subjects and How Well They Fit

Every subject below is mandatory in Ontario public elementary schools, except Native Languages. Fit means how much of the subject a child can learn and show on a phone, with a check the app can grade.

SubjectGradesFitWhy
Mathematics1–8HighAnswers are exact. Strand B is live. Strands C–F use the same generator and checks.
Language1–8High for reading, low for writingPhonics, words, grammar, and reading questions have one right answer. Free writing and speaking need a human or a speech model.
Science and Technology1–8HighEach grade has four fixed topics full of facts to sort, order, and label. Experiments can only be simulated.
Social Studies1–6MediumMaps, timelines, and vocabulary fit. Perspectives and inquiry don't have one answer. Grades 7–8 become History and Geography.
French as a Second LanguageCore 4–8, Immersion 1–8MediumVocabulary and reading fit. Needs French narration and a French UI first.
Health and Physical Education1–8LowOnly Healthy Living and the social-emotional skills fit. Movement can't be checked on a phone.
The Arts1–8LowMusic and visual arts basics fit. Dance and drama are performance.
Native Languages1–8, optionalOut of scopeEach language needs its own community, speakers, and recordings.

Kindergarten has one program for every subject. Its four strands map onto the subjects above, so K content comes with each subject rather than as a separate one.

2. Where the Programs Live

The Ministry of Education publishes every curriculum on the Curriculum and Resources site. Each grade lists its strands, overall expectations (A1, B2, …), and specific expectations (B2.1, …). The site renders in the browser, so content comes from its public API, the same way content/sources/ was built. The year is the one the API reports for each document.

3. Minigames Across Subjects

Math has eight minigames, plus the frog hops on the number line. Most other subjects need the same few moves: sort things into groups, put things in order, name parts of a picture, find a place on a map, and build a word or sentence from tiles. Each of these is one new minigame that many subjects share.

Existing pieces that carry over:

  • Story cards, ReadAlong, and tap items already handle reading questions and scenario choices.
  • The compare number line becomes a timeline: years instead of numbers for "which came first".
  • Stepping stones holds sounds or syllables, and the frog hops one stone per sound.
  • Arrays and grid carry area and measurement in Math E.

  • Sort it fits almost every topic: living or not, solid or liquid, natural or human-made, conductor or insulator, renewable or not.
  • Put in order fits life cycles, seasons, the water cycle, and food chains.
  • Label it fits plant parts, body systems, simple machines, and planets.
  • Simulations, such as a ramp for forces or a circuit for electricity, teach best but cost the most. Leave them for after the shared games.

French as a Second Language

Core French starts in Grade 4. Immersion starts in Grade 1, or earlier in some boards. The Language games carry over with French content: Tile builder for words, tap for listening, and story cards for reading. Two things come first:

  • A French narration voice. npm run content:voices can compare French voices the same way.
  • A French UI, which the product requirements already plan for launch.

Health and Physical Education

  • D Healthy Living fits: food choices, safety, and substance awareness, with Sort it and tap scenarios.
  • A Social-Emotional Learning fits as story choices: "What could Maya do?".
  • B and C are movement. A short move break between items could nod to them, without a check.
  • Parents can exempt a child from Human Development and Sexual Health in Strand D. Leave those expectations out, or behind a parent setting.

The Arts

  • C Music fits listening games: echo a rhythm, high or low pitch, name the instrument.
  • D Visual Arts fits the elements of design: a colour mixer, and Sort it for line, shape, and texture.
  • A Dance and B Drama are performance and stay out.
  • The 2009 document is the oldest one in use, so expect a revision.

Kindergarten

Only A6, Number Sense, is live. The other overall expectations follow the subject they match:

  • A1–A4 with Language.
  • A7–A10 with Math C–E.
  • B11–B13 with Science: Robot path and Sort it.
  • C14–C18 with Health, and D24–D25 with the Arts.
  • D19–D23 with Social Studies.

5. Suggested Order

flowchart LR
  M[Math C to F] --> L[Language K to 3]
  TB[Tile builder] --> L
  L --> S[Science]
  G[Sort it, Put in order, Label it] --> S
  S --> SS[Social Studies]
  MP[Map pin and timeline] --> SS
  L --> F[French]
  V[French voice and UI] --> F
  S --> H[Health, then Arts]
  1. Math Strands C–F. Same framework, generator, and checks. Turns the app from "Number" into "Math".
  2. Language, Grades K–3, Strands B2, B3, and C. The product requirements put Language in the MVP. Needs Tile builder.
  3. Science. Needs Sort it, Put in order, and Label it, which Social Studies and Health then reuse.
  4. Social Studies. Adds Map pin and the timeline.
  5. French. After a French voice and UI.
  6. Health, then the Arts. Only the parts listed above.

Adding a subject touches SUBJECTS and FRAMEWORKS in shared/src/skill.ts, a content/sources/ folder per subject, and the skill graph build. Packs already split by subject and grade.
